## What changed

Both simulators now import an immutable `simulation-bounds-v1`: minimum latency
250 ms, maximum configurable latency 60,000 ms and maximum participation 1%.
Values and existing financial behaviour are unchanged. The historical bar model
still permits a smaller participation assumption; paper uses the conservative
fixed 1% ceiling. `CostModel` keeps its existing serialized version and fields;
old manifest bytes are not rewritten. Code fingerprints bind the new source.

The bounds describe simulation. Portfolio admission and the independent execution
veto do not import them, share approval logic or acquire any new permission.
Existing pure indicators, signals and sizing remain shared. No duplicate state
machine, framework, service, migration or execution capability is introduced.

`tests/fixtures/parity/v1.json` contains literal hand-calculated expected results,
not snapshots generated by either engine. A strict fixture model reads Decimal
strings and rejects unknown fields. Tests run real `PaperExecution`, both risk
checks, `PaperExchange`, reconciliation and the journal against disposable
PostgreSQL. They also compare common executed-trade economics with the research
reference and exercise separate pure journal/unitisation cases.

## Independent arithmetic and chronology

The paper fixtures begin with £1,000, a £999 bid/£1,000 ask and one base unit of
displayed ask depth. A predeclared long decision has £50 stop distance. Sizing
permits exactly 0.01 BTC because of the 1% liquidity ceiling. Independent expected
order bounds are £1,001 entry limit, £950.90 trigger and £941.40 protective limit.
The quantity also passes the unchanged episode-risk and exposure checks.

At 249 ms no fill is possible. At 250 ms the order buys 0.01 BTC at £1,000,
paying £0.08 GBP fees; cash becomes £989.92. At a £1,000 mark, NAV is £999.92 and
unit price is 0.99992. A research comparator with a £10.08 fee-inclusive budget
executes the same trade, yielding exactly those quantities, fees, NAV and units.
This isolates common executed economics; it is not evidence of identical strategy
timing, sizing mandates or market paths.

All stop cases trigger at 500 ms on a £950 bid. That event cannot fill the stop.
The order cannot match before 750 ms. The literal paths cover:

| Case | Admissible later evidence | Independent result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recovered bid | £960 bid at 749 ms, then 750 ms | No early fill; 0.01 sold at £960 at 750 ms. Cash/NAV £999.4432; total fees £0.1568. |
| Rapid gap | £800 bids after triggering | Zero sales; 0.01 BTC remains protected. Cash £989.92, NAV £997.92. No invented sale below £941.40. |
| No executable liquidity | Bids recover to £960/£970 with 0.0000001 displayed base units | After the 1% ceiling and quantity rounding, zero sales; 0.01 BTC remains protected. Final NAV £999.62. A quoted price alone supplies no executable volume. |
| Partial residual | £960 bid with 0.4 displayed base units, gap to £800, then £970 with ample depth | Sell 0.004 at £960, retain 0.006 and protection through the gap, then sell 0.006 at £970. Final cash/NAV £999.50272; total fees £0.15728. |

For example, the partial sale adds `0.004 × 960 × (1 − 0.008) = £3.80928`
to cash. The final sale adds `0.006 × 970 × (1 − 0.008) = £5.77344`.
Every step checks cash, owned inventory, cumulative fills, actual fees, NAV and
residual protection. Repeated observations/reconciliation leave quantities,
financial postings and order economics unchanged. A reconciliation event may
advance the operational version; that is not a duplicate financial posting.
Later events cannot rewrite an earlier authoritative fill.

Further independent examples cover:

- A 0.00001 BTC fee on a 0.01 BTC acquisition at £1,000: owned quantity 0.00999,
  cost basis £9.99 and GBP cash £990. Selling 0.004 at £1,200 with £0.0384 GBP fees
  leaves 0.00599 BTC, £5.99 basis and £994.7616 cash; gross realised gain is £0.80.
  This is accounting basis, not an owner-wide tax computation.
- An existing £1,000 holding falling 10%: unit price 0.9 and both drawdown latches
  set. Contributing £90 issues 100 units and withdrawing £45 redeems 50; unit price
  and latches remain unchanged. Journal rebuilds equal incremental balances.
  Serialisation/report boundaries preserve state and the policy epoch. A subsequent
  exact UTC midnight clears only the daily suspension, never the hard/soft latch.
  This reporting fixture is not a newly risk-admitted 100%-exposure position.
- A closed slow-signal prefix is unavailable at close+9.999 seconds and valid at
  close+10 seconds. Future candles cannot be admitted to the earlier decision.
  Dispatch at approval+1.999 seconds is valid; equality at +2 seconds is rejected
  without a venue submission. Forged affirmative approval economics cannot bypass
  the independent planned-loss veto.

The executable drawdown probe uses observations at unit prices 0.9 intraday and
1.0 at day-end. The required all-observation drawdown is 10%; the legacy daily
metric reports zero. The entry probe shows a valid long at close+10 seconds but
no legacy fill during that first decision bar. The stop probe uses a prescribed
long decision, a 0.01 BTC entry, a £950 trigger and £940.50 limit. When a later
bid is £960, the legacy minimum-price stress sells at £940.50, producing
£999.24976 NAV instead of the paper recovered-bid case's £999.4432. Paper's entry
limit and tick-rounded protection differ as declared above. These probes name the
limitations without claiming that a bar and book observation are interchangeable.
